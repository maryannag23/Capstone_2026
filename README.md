# Capstone_2026
This project analyzed retail marketing campaign data to identify customer behaviors and demographic trends associated with successful campaign responses. The primary goal was to transform transaction data into actionable business insights that could support more strategic marketing decisions and improve promotional effectiveness. The target audience for this analysis includes retail marketing teams and business leadership seeking to better understand customer purchasing patterns and optimize return on investment for marketing campaigns. The dataset contained 2,241 rows and 28 columns, and analysis was conducted using Python, Tableau, and Excel.

Before analysis, the dataset required several cleaning and preprocessing steps. Missing income values were identified and handled using Python, while inconsistencies in marital status, education categories, and date formatting were standardized to improve data quality. Outliers were also reviewed, including unrealistic birth years and unusually high income values that likely resulted from data-entry errors. Statistical analysis methods included correlation analysis, heat maps, scatterplots, and comparative visualizations to explore relationships between income, age, purchasing behavior, and campaign response rates. The analysis found that customers with incomes below $100,000 represented the majority of the dataset, with the largest concentration of customers between ages 45 and 70. Additionally, customers with incomes between approximately $75,000 and $95,000 and moderate store visit frequency showed higher campaign response rates.

Several key findings emerged from the analysis. Customers who made more online purchases demonstrated higher response rates to marketing campaigns, suggesting that digital marketing strategies should be prioritized. Wine purchasers were also more likely to accept promotional offers, indicating that this customer segment may provide strong opportunities for targeted campaigns. Additionally, divorced, single, and widowed customers responded at higher rates than married individuals, suggesting that people living alone may engage differently with marketing promotions. Based on these findings, the company should focus future campaigns on digital engagement, personalized promotions, and high-performing customer segments. Future analysis could expand into predictive modeling and customer segmentation to improve campaign forecasting and long-term marketing effectiveness.
